title: Building GridSchool

I started GridSchool because I kept watching capable people who could write a program fail hiring loops that asked for proof they had never been taught to produce. When the AI wave hit, it got worse. The tools changed faster than anyone could explain them, and it got harder for teams to tell who was good.

GridSchool went through several shapes before the current one. The first version was a 12-month residency with a fixed evidence curriculum and a shared multiplayer world where students shipped changes. I retired both. The current version is an 8-week route of teaching and review, with weekly 1:1s that continue until a company hires the student.

The biggest change in GridSchool was moving from a fixed curriculum to a map. Each student gets a route built around their goal, drafted by the map builder from a module library and rebuilt when something happens: a rejection, a new target, an interview that went sideways. My notes on why the map matters cover the reasoning.

The second big change was automating the job search itself. I built the Hire engine so each student gets a daily run ordered by evidence, with referrals found in their own LinkedIn connections export, role packs, follow-ups, and fresh roles. I applied the same kind of systems I build for code to the busywork of a job search, so students spend their hours on work and people.

GridSchool is still being tuned with the founding students, and the offer says so. The founding terms rise once the founding five have defended in front of outside engineers. I would rather say plainly that the method is new than pretend it is a finished institution.
