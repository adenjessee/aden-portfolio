title: Why GridSchool is a map
---

GridSchool is built around a map because every engineer starts somewhere different and wants a different job. A fixed curriculum assumes the same start and the same goal for everyone. A map lets me build a route from where a student actually stands to the role they want.

On GridSchool, the student's home is the map itself. Each capability is a node on a grid, and the nodes form the student's route. One node is open at a time, and there is always a clear next node. When I add something new to GridSchool, it becomes a node on the map.

I chose one open node at a time on purpose. People who are stuck in a job search usually have too many things to do and no clear next step. A single open node removes that question. The student always knows what to work on today.

A GridSchool map changes when life changes. A rejection, a new target company, or an interview that went sideways all feed back into the route, and the map builder drafts the new version for me to review. The plan stays tied to what is actually happening.

The map is also GridSchool's brand. I work on graphs of code all day: nodes, edges, and what a change reaches. A student's route is a graph too, with capabilities as nodes and the path to a hire as the edges.
