title: How I became an engineer
---

I became a software engineer before the AI era. I learned by writing systems from scratch and shipping software that people depended on, before large language models could write any of it for me.

I did not come to graph engineering from a research lab. I came to it from shipping software and then having to explain why a change in one file broke something three folders away. The first time you inherit a system you did not design, a portfolio of tutorial projects starts to look fake. Nothing in those projects prepared me for finding out what a change would reach.

That experience shaped the question I still design for. Can you say what you touched, what else it reaches, and how you would know if you were wrong? GridSeak is my tool for answering it about code. GridSchool is how I teach other engineers to answer it about their own work.

When AI coding tools arrived, the question got more urgent. Code started appearing faster than anyone could read it, and agents began reporting success on changes they had not verified. Having learned to build without those tools, I could see where they were strong and where they were bluffing, and I built my work around checking them.
