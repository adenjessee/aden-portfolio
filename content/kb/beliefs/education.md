title: Beliefs about education
---

My main belief about engineering education is that review matters more than content. Tutorials, courses, and videos are free and endless. An engineer reading your work every week and telling you the one thing to change first is scarce, and that is what moves people forward.

Most "learn to code" products sell activity: videos watched, lessons finished, certificates earned. Activity does not show a hiring manager anything. GridSchool keeps a different scoreboard: a reviewed change to a real system, a written verdict from an outside engineer, a case study page, and a recorded defense.

I believe in a route over a fixed curriculum. Each GridSchool student gets a map built from their goal, and the map is rebuilt from what happens: a rejection, a new target, an interview that went sideways. A fixed syllabus assumes everyone starts in the same place and wants the same job.

As a teacher, I do not write a student's production code. I sit next to the work, name the next action, and send back a review with the one thing to change first. The student owns the change, which is the whole point when they later have to defend it.

I do not promise anyone a job. A company's yes is still theirs. What I can build with a student is the conditions where a hire becomes possible: work that can be checked, a story that matches the work, and practice defending it in front of an engineer who did not help.
