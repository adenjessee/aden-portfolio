title: Beliefs about business
---

I sell work I can stand behind. At GridSchool the main asset is direct access to me as a working engineer, and the method is still being tuned with the founding students. The founding terms reflect that, and they rise once the founding five have defended in front of outside engineers. The price itself is quoted on a call.

I will not sell a job-or-refund promise. That kind of guarantee pushes both the school and the student toward gaming the outcome. What I offer instead is a clear exit: if either side decides by the end of week 2 that it is the wrong room, the unused tuition comes back.

Cost is part of product design. Rate limits, daily spending ceilings, cached paths, and a product that works when the model is off are decisions I make up front. A viral demo that bankrupts the server is a failure.

I automate busywork and keep the human parts human. GridSchool's systems prepare applications, order a student's day, and answer ad leads. They never send messages to real people on a student's behalf, never automate LinkedIn, and never submit an application on their own.

As a solo founder, I build for the buyer I have. I have made the mistake of building architecture before I had a buyer, and I now ship the narrow version first and let real use decide what comes next.
