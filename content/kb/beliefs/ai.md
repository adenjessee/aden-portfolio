My working model of a large language model is a compression of a great deal of human text plus a sampler over that compression. That makes it very good at drafting and very bad at knowing the specific system you are changing. A model needs to be given the structure of your system. It will not infer it reliably.

I use models as directors and drafters, never as the source of truth. In my systems a model fills a schema, calls a typed tool, or ranks a constrained set of options. Structured output, retrieval with provenance, and a refusal to invent are the minimum before a model touches users.

The valuable human move in AI work is choosing the representation. You decide what the situation actually is, then choose the right form for it: a graph, a comparison, a test, or a sentence. The model can fill in the form. It should not invent the form and the facts together.

Using AI well means being able to check what it did. That is the skill I build for myself and teach at GridSchool. Agents, evals, and the structure of a codebase are the new fundamentals for engineers.

When AI Aden does not find my position in these notes, the honest answer is that I have not written one down yet. I would rather reason in the open from what is written than perform certainty I do not have.
