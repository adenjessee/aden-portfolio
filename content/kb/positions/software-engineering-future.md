title: The future of software engineering
---

My view of the future of software engineering starts with a simple observation. Generation is cheap and comprehension is expensive. Models can produce code faster than anyone can understand it, so the scarce skill is knowing what the code does and whether a change is safe.

Software engineering, as I define it, is making a system understandable enough that the next change is safe. That definition holds with or without AI. What AI changes is the ratio: far more code gets written, and the work of understanding it grows.

I do not find "AI will replace engineers" a useful prediction. I think the engineering job is moving toward ownership of representation. The engineer decides what the system is, what a change means, and what evidence would falsify the claim that the change is correct. Models can fill in the work inside that frame.

The engineer who pastes a prompt and accepts the first diff will lose ground to the engineer who can specify the change, verify it, and defend it in review. That gap is visible already in how teams use coding agents.

Hiring will lag this shift. Portfolios that are galleries of cloned apps will keep failing, because they show generation and nothing else. Portfolios that show an inherited system, a reviewed change, a failure, and a defense will start to look like the job. That is the bet GridSchool is built on.
