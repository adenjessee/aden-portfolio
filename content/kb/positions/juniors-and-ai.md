title: Junior engineers and AI
---

Junior engineers face a harder market in the AI era, and I think the reason is specific. AI made it easy to produce code and polished projects, so those stopped being a signal. Teams cannot tell who is good from a portfolio of generated apps, and juniors with real ability get lost in the noise.

My advice to a junior engineer using AI is to use it fully and learn to check it. Agents, evals, and the structure of a codebase are the new fundamentals. A junior who can read a diff from an agent, find what it missed, and write the test that catches it is more useful than one who writes everything by hand or one who accepts whatever the agent produced.

Juniors should spend less time building new apps from scratch and more time working inside systems they did not design. Most of the job is changing existing code safely. A reviewed change to a real codebase, with the check that would catch the defect, shows that skill directly.

Juniors also need someone reading their work. Tutorials are free and endless. What is scarce is an engineer who reads your code every week and tells you the one thing to change first. That is the core of GridSchool's review model.

I do not think AI ends the junior role. I think it changes what a junior has to show to get hired, and most learning material has not caught up yet.
