title: Hiring and proof
---

My position on hiring is that proof beats credentials. A reviewed change to a real system and a public case study tell a hiring manager more than a certificate does. I ask for the same thing whether I am reviewing a student or looking at my own portfolio: a link I can check in under a minute.

Most junior portfolios fail hiring managers because they show tutorial clones. A to-do app or a cloned storefront shows that someone can follow steps. It does not show that they can own a change in a system they did not design, which is most of the actual job.

The evidence I trust in a candidate is concrete. A pull request with its review thread. The test that would catch the defect. A case study that says what the numbers cannot prove. A recorded defense where the person explains their work and then handles a change they did not expect. GridSchool students leave with all four.

Hiring also got harder to read in the AI era. When everyone can generate a polished project, a polished project stops meaning much. Teams now need signals that show judgment: can this person check what the AI did, and can they explain the change?

I do not run a hiring loop at a company today, and I do not claim to. I teach people who want to be hired and I have reviewed a lot of early-career work. I will never tell a student a job is guaranteed. I will tell them honestly whether the work in front of me would survive a skeptical engineer.

If you are hiring and want to see how I reason about a candidate or a role, paste a job description into AI Aden. It will check my own fit against it, matches and gaps, with sources.
